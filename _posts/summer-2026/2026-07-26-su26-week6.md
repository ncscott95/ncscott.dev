It’s time for the first true milestone of the project! Today, I sent out my first demo of the rope dart system for initial playtesting after a long week of working on pixel art animations and updating the game systems. 


### A (Very) Rough Draft

The goal for this demo was to be a proof-of-concept of the game’s core rope dart system. Currently, the demo allows the player to spin and cast the dart, with added options to twine to an elbow before casting. I think that this achieves a decent amount of my original goal, though there are a few major parts of the system that didn’t make it. 

The biggest missing interaction group is “wraps,” which allow the player to hold part of the rope in place while performing other actions, before releasing it all at once for a more powerful finisher. Only a few days ago, I realized I had made a major assumption that these wraps could be released for a cast at any time. This is not accurate, as in most cases this will just result in getting tangled in the rope. Instead, I needed to create a system for detecting when a wrap has been “re-traced,” and only then can it be properly released. With the demo deadline so close and a lot of artwork still left to be done, I decided it was better to leave this for the next milestone. 

On a more positive note, the demo contains my first attempts at pixel art animation! While the player character itself is not animated, all of the rope dart swings are animated frame-by-frame. I was incredibly tempted to go back to the procedural motion I was using in the initial prototype, but I’m happy with how this turned out. I still have a lot of work to do with refining the character, especially their hands, but I’m proud of my progress so far. 

Please feel free to play the demo, available to play in your web browser on [Itch.io](https://valius95.itch.io/rope-dart-demo){:target="_blank"}! 


### New Toys

I recently picked up some new equipment to boost my development and branch out into some of the fields I’m less experienced in! For art, I got my first drawing tablet, while for music, I got my first MIDI controller (shoutout Ethan, Jess, and Aidan!). It’s been a bit of a challenge adjusting to these new tools, but equally fun and rewarding to just mess around with them. 

I immediately put the drawing tablet to work while working on the pixel art animations for the demo, and I feel it greatly improved by drawing speed (while also being far more comfortable than drawing with a mouse!). I haven’t made anything concrete yet with the MIDI keyboard, though I did spend a few hours browsing free VSTs. I’m looking forward to getting started on the game’s soundtrack next week!
