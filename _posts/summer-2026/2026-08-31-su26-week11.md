This week, I continued to make progress on player animations. After almost 30 hours in Aseprite in the past few weeks, I finally feel like I’m hitting my stride and starting to finish some assets! 


### Animation Progress

I have completed my first full set of animations! The player can now spin the rope, shoot it out with their hand or elbow, and pull it back to start spinning in the opposite direction. With these complete, the player can play through a complete sequence of fully animated moves!

### Simplifying Rope Animation

Two weeks ago, I wrote about how I had to cut out a significant number of possible rope dart moves for the sake of scope. In addition to reducing the number of unique player animations I have to draw, it has also simplified my process for animating the rope itself. 

Before making cuts, the player could have made hundreds of different configurations of the rope, each with varying paths around the body and slack lengths. As such, I had originally split the visuals for the player and the rope into separate systems (two Animator Controller components, for instance). I was even toying with a procedural rope system with simulated physics and a pixel-perfect line renderer, which could be layered on top of a standard sprite animation for the player. However, now that gameplay has been reduced to a small set of predefined sequences, I don’t have to worry as much about procedural animation. The only thing that I’m still planning on keeping modular is how wraps are shown. 


### The Wrap Layer

The problem I have to solve is how to show different rope wraps (around the waist, over the shoulder, around the neck, etc.) while performing arbitrary moves. Here is the new idea. Most of the player’s body will still be animated using one sprite with an Animator Controller. However, the arms will be moved to a new layer on top of the existing layer, which creates a space between the two for a new middle layer. This middle layer will be where any wrapped rope is shown, and I can switch the sprite to match whatever wrap the player makes. 

On the art side, this means making animations for each possible wrap that moves with the body in all possible animations. While this may sound like a lot of art, it’s actually quite doable. By constraining the player to only use preset sequences of moves, there are only 4 possible wrap types: belt (hip to hip), necklace (shoulder to shoulder), dragon (armpit to opposite shoulder), and scorpion (shoulder to opposite armpit). Additionally, each of these frames is basically just a one-pixel-wide line across the body, and it will only be shifted by a pixel or two to account for player motion in most cases. 


### Coming Up

Now that I have a functional set of animations, I’m going to take a brief break from art to implement this new modular wrap system and test the animations in an actual level. Once this is done, it’ll be back to Aseprite to complete the player wrap animations and level sprites (background, targets, etc.). If all goes well, I will be finished with gameplay art for an absolute minimum viable product by the end of this week!
