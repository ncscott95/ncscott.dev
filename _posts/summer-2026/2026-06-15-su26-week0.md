Hello, and welcome to my first devlog! This is where I’ll be posting weekly progress updates on my personal game project that I will be working on during this summer. 


### About Me

My name is Nathan Scott, and I have just completed my second year as a Computer Science student at UC San Diego. I currently serve as a Programming Lead for the school’s [Video Game Development Club (VGDC)](https://www.vgdc.dev/){:target="_blank"} and have been developing games as a hobby since 8th grade. Most of my experience has come from game jams and club projects, where I primarily work as a Unity developer and C# programmer. You can check out some of my prior work on my [itch.io](https://valius95.itch.io/){:target="_blank"} profile! 


### A Summer “Internship”

Having really enjoyed making games with VGDC, I have become increasingly interested in exploring indie game development as a career. So, going into summer without an internship lined up, I decided that now would be a good time to build a proper portfolio piece while also getting some experience with the entire indie dev process. And so, a grand plan: 14 weeks, 40 hours per week, and a game published on Steam before the start of the next school year. 


### Project Goals

While the end result of a published game and portfolio piece are certainly important goals for me, my biggest focus for this project is on the development process itself, and developing the multitude of skills that come with it. 

First and foremost, this will be a test of focus and self-discipline. I want to hold myself to full-time hours as much as realistically possible (taking breaks for family vacation of course), and to set and meet regular deadlines for prototypes, playtests, and publication. 

Secondly, I want to take this as an opportunity to branch out and get more comfortable with other skills besides programming. As such, I am committing to making 100% of the assets in the game, from art to music to UI to writing. Even beyond creative skills, I want to improve my project management, organization, and communication, such as through these very devlogs! 

Finally, if I am to pursue game development as a career in the future, I will need to learn what goes into publishing a successful game. While I have not yet decided if I will make the game free to play or not, I still want to get some experience marketing the game, building a social media presence, and creating anticipation for the game’s release. 


### Project Overview

Since I plan on doing and learning so many different things over the course of this project, I want to keep the project’s scope relatively small. Currently, I am envisioning a scope similar to that of games that I have made in two-week game jams with teams of roughly 6 people. While this definitely errs on the side of caution, I want to make sure that I have plenty of time to learn, fail, and improve, especially for my first time making game art and music. This smaller scope will also (hopefully) constrain my game design and force me to focus on a few key mechanics, rather than falling for feature creep. 

To keep myself organized and on track, I will be using a project management workflow loosely based on Agile systems I have used in past projects. I will be running seven two-week sprints, each ending with a major project milestone. Additionally, I will be posting a devlog here weekly on Sundays, detailing progress and challenges while also keeping myself publicly accountable. 


### Coming Up

While I initially wanted to have solidified the game’s concept by today, I have been struggling to decide on which game to make and how to make sure it will best match the goals I outlined above. So, my goal for this first week is to flesh out each of my ideas, create quick proofs of concept for each one, and commit to a final idea by this Sunday. The remaining week of Sprint 1 will hopefully then be spent creating an initial playable prototype. 

That’s all for this first devlog, thanks for reading!
