This week, I had to make some tough decisions regarding the scope and design of the game, as well as for the game’s story inspiration and protagonist design. 


### Demo 2 Feedback

Thank you to everyone who played last week’s demo! The feedback I received was very insightful despite the rough demo, particularly regarding player intuition. Across the board, the biggest piece of feedback I received was that controlling the rope dart was simply too confusing for people who didn’t already understand the mechanics of a rope dart. This revealed a key issue about the direction I was taking the game in. 

Essentially, I was trying too hard to design the game to be a perfect representation of a real rope dart. While this made sense to me in the design phase, I had overlooked just how complicated the various moves can get, especially once you start adding in how the rope’s spin direction and player’s facing direction can cause the same movement to do completely different things. If this sounds familiar, this is basically the same issue I experienced with [designing the underlying code system](https://ncscott.dev/summer-2026/2026/08/02/su26-week7.html){:target="_blank"}, except now it's the player’s brain that has to account for an exponentially increasing number of moves as they progress through the game. However, re-using my solution of basing the game controls around body parts seems to be making things even more complicated and difficult to convey to players. 


### Reducing Complexity (and Art!)

In order to reduce this complexity, I, ironically, have decided to shift game controls away from fully atomic body-based movements and towards a system more akin to classic fighting games, where a short series of inputs corresponds to preset moves. This does go against one of my original core ideas for the game, that of giving the player total control over the dart, but I think it makes the game much more intuitive. 

This simplification also addresses a few other issues that I had only just started to identify. First of all, the demo feedback had started forcing me to think about how I was going to teach such a complex system to a player. In the old system, I basically would have been giving a crash course on how a real rope dart works, which is something I was already struggling to do with my friends over voice call. With this more simplified approach, I can offer the player an atomic “ability” that can be chained with other moves under most circumstances, which is both more familiar to players of similar games and more intuitive for inexperienced gamers. 

Secondly, once I had started listing out all of the animations I needed to make for the player, it became clear that accounting for all of the different permutations of player and rope direction and different sequences of body parts were going to completely overwhelm my beginner art skills. So, in combination with reducing the complexity of player moves, I am removing the player’s ability to rotate the character away from the camera. This effectively halves the amount of art I need to make, while also removing an entire axis of motion for the player to worry about. 


### Thematic Research and Concepts, Continued

I spent a good deal of time this week trying to lock down my inspirations for the game’s story, art, characters, and music. As of now, I have mostly centered around the Chinese martial fantasy genres of [wuxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxia){:target="_blank"} and [xianxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianxia){:target="_blank"}, which loosely correspond to low and high fantasy, respectively. I think I’m leaning more towards xianxia for my project, as it focuses less on a historical period and more on mythology, which I just find more interesting. 

For the game’s main conflict, I am currently working with an idea centered around an incursion of ghosts and evil spirits from the underworld into the mortal plane. The main antagonist would be a powerful evil spirit, possibly inspired by [Chiyou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiyou){:target="_blank"}, who seeks to permanently open the *[Guimen guan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gate_of_the_Ghosts){:target="_blank"}*, or gate to the underworld. 

For the main protagonist, I have settled on a design inspired by various Chinese martial arts practitioners, particularly those of Shaolin and Wudang kung fu. As extra character flavor and to tie into a more mythological setting, I am considering giving the character a glowing magical rope dart to cleanse the evil spirits, and possibly a matching glowing arm tattoo. 


### Coming Up

Now that I have basic outlines for the main character and setting, it’s finally time to stop procrastinating and start working on final player animations. I can see myself struggling a decent amount on this, so I think a decent goal for myself will be to finish a first pass of all player animations by the end of the next two weeks.
