This week, my work was split between adding features to the rope dart system and doing research to start concepting for the game’s setting, themes, and characters. 


### Prototype Demo, Part 2

After the first demo two weeks ago, I wanted this iteration to include all possible core rope dart actions, at least in some capacity. I believe that I have achieved this, with this week’s additions of wrapping and uncoiling. While I didn’t have time to complete the animations for these features, you can technically perform them in the new version of the demo, albeit with little visual indication and a multitude of bugs. 

Adding the uncoiling feature in particular was one of the most difficult and frustrating features I have added so far. When the player twines the rope around their elbow or neck, the dart will spiral around that part of the body until it runs out of slack, at which point it is considered “stalled.” From a stall, the user should be able to reverse the spin, causing the dart to uncoil, spiraling outwards as it unwraps. Implementing this feature meant adding detection for when the dart rotates past a certain angle, then triggering a new binding to keep the spiral going automatically. It also meant creating a new set of boolean flags to differentiate between a neck twine and a dragon wrap, since both actions end at the player’s neck, but only the neck twine should automatically coil. 

As I mentioned above, there are still plenty of bugs with this system and the lack of transition animations makes it pretty unintuitive. However, I’m happy with my progress so far, and I’m looking forward to iterating on it! You can play the demo now on [Itch.io](https://valius95.itch.io/rope-dart-demo){:target="_blank"}! 


### Thematic Research and Concepts

Shifting to the creative side of things, I have started worldbuilding and working on concept art for the main character. However, I quickly realized that I didn’t have much of a core thematic idea to work around. So, I spent some time researching Chinese mythology and folk religion for inspiration. 

So far, I have been toying around with the idea of the [Wǔfāng Shàngdì](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wufang_Shangdi){:target="_blank"}, or five heavenly deities associated with the five phases of creation. Each deity is also associated with a distinct color, fundamental element, and animal, and I think these lend themselves well to partitioning the game into different sets of levels that can have distinct mechanics, art, and music. From here, I need to spend some more time to craft a protagonist that would reasonably find themselves interacting with these five deities in a way that is also respectful of existing stories and traditions.
