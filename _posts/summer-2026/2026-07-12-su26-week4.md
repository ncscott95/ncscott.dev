This week, I spent most of my time prototyping the rope dart combat system, while also setting up my project management workflow and preparing for more long-term development. 


### Rope Dart Prototype Progress

Most of my work this week was spent on the “twining” and “wrapping” mechanics of the rope dart. A twine is when the player uses another part of their body, such as an elbow, shoulder, or knee, to change the speed and direction of the dart. My initial mockup allowed for simple shifting between arbitrary points, but I realized it couldn’t account for the more complicated states and variations that I will need. So, I spent a while making a new state graph where different twines are represented as traversing between nodes in the graph. 

The second mechanic I worked on was rope wraps, or specific twines that can be held in place to create loops around the body. As part of the game’s mechanics, I think it would be fun if each wrap grants the player a unique buff while it is held, as well as a unique bonus when it is released. After switching to the new state graph system, adding wraps was as simple as creating a new controller input and adding a bool to each node in the graph to check if a wrap can be held at that node. 


### Reconsidering Scope

As I was working on this combat prototype, I realized just how large of a game I was setting myself up for. Based on my current progress, I foresee spending the next two weeks on the rope dart system alone, let alone player movement and enemy attacks and combat AI. So, as of now, I am making the decision to majorly alter the design and scope of the project. 

I am shifting away from a beat-’em-up combat game to a score-based game purely focused on creating combos. For now, I’m envisioning a few different level objectives, such as longest combo or hitting a sequence of targets. While this is a pretty big departure from my original vision for the game, I think this will greatly reduce the scope for both programming and art while allowing me to explore the rope dart mechanic more deeply without needing to worry about combat and enemy balancing.

Within GitHub Projects, I am using top-level issues to represent user stories, and sub-issues to represent tasks.
