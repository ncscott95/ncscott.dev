I’m on vacation! Naturally, work this week was a bit slower than usual, but I still made decent progress on research and design. 


### Refining Design: Levels

I have started planning out the core game loop and basic progression systems. Right now, I am targeting 8 levels divided into 4 pairs, each consisting of one “horde” level and one “boss” level. Each pair will have a distinct environment and theme that is shared between the two levels. If scope allows, I would also like to have an additional final boss level once the player has cleared all 8 levels with a high enough score, and completing this final level would result in the completion of the game. 

I am also still deciding what gameplay should look like between levels. On one hand, a level select menu would be relatively simple to implement. On the other hand, I quite enjoy when games have explorable in-game “hub” levels. In particular, I think a hub level could be a good space for the player to experiment with combat in a stress-free environment. It will require more work, however, so this is a pretty low-priority item for now. 


### Refining Design: Progression

Thinking more about player skill progression, I am currently toying with two different approaches to advancement: “discovery” and “unlocks.” In the “discovery” system, all possible rope dart actions are immediately available for the player to use, they just don’t know how to use them yet. If the player stumbles across a powerful combo early on, that experimentation is rewarded. I would also reward high level scores with hints or guides to teach the player new moves as they progress through the game. 

On the other hand, the “unlocks” system only allows a limited moveset at the beginning of the game, and locked moves won’t work until the player unlocks them. In this idea, I think moves could be unlocked either linearly by completing levels, or through some kind of skill tree. Either way, replaying earlier levels would keep the player’s unlocked moves, allowing them to score higher with better combos. 

I am currently leaning in favor of the “discovery” system since I want to reward experimentation, but I will continue developing the “unlocks” idea and try to get both versions into the hands of playtesters for my early prototypes. 


### Thematic Research

Because the rope dart is tightly linked with Chinese martial arts, which in turn has strong cultural significance, I want to make sure that the game’s setting is a respectful representation rather than an appropriation or commodification. As such, I have started doing research into the histories of Chinese martial arts and Buddhism in China and Nepal. I have been exploring different novels and myths as possible options for narrative inspiration, instead of creating a story from scratch. 

Additionally, I have started on my visual mood board! I have really been liking the idea of a minimal style similar to the Alto’s games applied to traditional Chinese ink landscapes. I’m not yet sure how this will work, though, so more exploration is required.
